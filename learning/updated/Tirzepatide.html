Prescription medicine guide

Tirzepatide

What the research shows about a GIP and GLP-1 receptor activator, and where the evidence stops

27 November 2026

What it is

Tirzepatide is a prescription medicine that activates two gut hormone receptors, GIP and GLP-1. It is supplied in different injection presentations, and the prescription and the instructions for the exact device govern what a person should do.

A clinic or pharmacist can explain which presentation has been supplied and what to check on its label. This guide stays with the research and leaves the practical steps to those instructions.

[3]

1. The studies at a glance

The main trial, SURMOUNT-1, randomized 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related complication, excluding people with diabetes. Over 72 weeks it reported average weight reductions of roughly 15% to 21% across the tirzepatide groups against 3.1% with placebo.

A body composition substudy analyzed 160 people who had scans at the start and end of the trial. Fat mass and lean mass both fell in the tirzepatide group. A subset is not the whole trial, and lean mass is not the same thing as muscle strength.

What each kind of evidence can and cannot tell you

EvidenceWhat it can tell youWhat it cannot tell you

2. Forms and handling

The medicine comes in more than one injection presentation. The prescription and the specific device instructions set out what to do, and a pharmacist or prescriber can walk through the exact presentation and the label checks that matter.

This guide does not cover home mixing, transferring between containers, counting clicks or converting to generic syringe units. Those belong to the instructions for the product in hand.

[3]

3. Side effects

Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and constipation are reported. Severe persistent abdominal pain, or being unable to keep fluids down, warrants prompt care. Breathing difficulty, swelling of the throat or tongue, or collapse require emergency help.

People using insulin or sulfonylureas, and people using oral hormonal contraceptives, should raise this with a clinician, because product directions include interactions or precautions.

When to get help quickly

  • Severe persistent abdominal pain, or being unable to keep fluids down, warrants prompt care.
  • Breathing difficulty, swelling of the throat or tongue, or collapse require emergency help.

4. How it has been studied

SURMOUNT-1 was a randomized trial in adults with obesity or overweight and a weight-related complication, with diabetes excluded. It ran for 72 weeks and compared tirzepatide groups with placebo on average weight change.

The body composition substudy sat inside that trial and looked at scans in 160 people. It describes what happened to fat and lean mass across the group, not what will happen to one person.

[1] [2]

5. What the evidence actually shows

The trial shows that, on average, the tirzepatide groups lost considerably more weight than the placebo group over 72 weeks. The substudy shows that the loss included both fat and lean mass.

The evidence does not promise an individual result. It also does not show that lean mass changes equal muscle strength changes. Nutrition and activity remain conversations with a care team.

[1] [2]

6. Who should be cautious

The trial population was adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related complication, and people with diabetes were excluded, so the trial results do not directly describe that group. People using insulin, sulfonylureas or oral hormonal contraceptives have product directions to check with a clinician.

Anyone with severe persistent abdominal pain or inability to keep fluids down should seek prompt care, and breathing difficulty, throat or tongue swelling or collapse mean emergency help.

7. Monitoring

The studies monitored weight and, in the substudy, body composition by scan. They do not set out a personal monitoring plan.

In practice, what gets checked and how often is agreed with the prescriber, guided by the exact prescription and the product instructions.

[1] [2] [3]

8. Food and muscle

The body composition substudy found that lean mass fell alongside fat mass in the tirzepatide group. Lean mass is not identical to muscle strength, so the scans alone cannot say how strong someone is.

How food and activity fit around the medicine is a care-team discussion. The supplied evidence does not prescribe a particular eating or exercise plan.

[2]

9. Timing and interruptions

The timing of doses follows the exact prescription instructions. When something is uncertain, such as an interruption or a departure from the usual routine, the prescriber is the person to ask.

This guide deliberately avoids intervals and schedules, because they belong to the prescription and the device instructions rather than to a general guide.

[3]

10. Common mistakes

A common mistake is treating the average trial result as a personal forecast. Another is reading the body composition substudy as if it covered the whole trial or proved a change in muscle strength.

A third is leaning on general guidance for handling and timing instead of the instructions for the exact product and the prescriber's advice. A fourth is forgetting to mention insulin, sulfonylureas or oral hormonal contraceptives to a clinician.

11. FAQ

Does the trial guarantee I will lose the same amount?
No. The trial reports averages across groups over 72 weeks, and an individual result can sit above or below those averages.
Did the substudy show muscle loss?
It showed lean mass fell along with fat mass in the tirzepatide group. Lean mass is not the same as muscle strength, so the scans do not settle that question.
Were people with diabetes included?
No. SURMOUNT-1 excluded people with diabetes, so its results do not directly describe that group.
Where do I find the practical steps?
In the instructions for the exact product and from a pharmacist or prescriber, since presentations differ.

12. Plain-English glossary

Randomized trial
A study where people are assigned by chance to different groups, so the groups can be compared fairly.
Placebo
A comparison treatment with no active medicine, used to show what happens without it.
Substudy
A smaller piece of research nested inside a larger trial, looking at a narrower question in fewer people.
Lean mass
The part of body weight that is not fat. It is not the same as muscle strength.
