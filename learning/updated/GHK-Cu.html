Research guide

GHK-Cu

What the evidence says about a copper tripeptide complex

27 November 2026

What it is

GHK-Cu is a copper tripeptide complex. The peptide part is made of three amino acids, glycine, histidine and lysine, and the copper is bound to it.

The evidence in this guide comes from a topical skin study. Topical skin products and injectable preparations are separate, so keep them apart when you read or compare them.

[1]

1. The studies at a glance

One randomized controlled study looked at topical regimens after carbon dioxide laser resurfacing around the mouth. Thirteen people completed two topical-regimen groups. At 12 weeks, satisfaction favored the GHK-Cu regimen. Objective evaluations did not find a significant between-group difference in redness resolution, wrinkles or overall skin quality.

The study tested whole topical regimens in one procedure setting. It does not show the effect of every formulation, routine cosmetic use outside that setting, or injectable GHK-Cu.

2. Forms and handling

The supplied evidence covers a topical regimen, not an injectable product. For an actual topical cosmetic, its ingredient list and instructions are the useful reference.

Because products differ, no single handling rule applies to all of them. Read the label of the exact product you have and follow what it says.

[1]

3. Side effects

The study was small and focused on satisfaction and objective skin measures, so it does not give a safety rate. Persistent irritation is worth discussing with a clinician.

The supplied evidence does not describe a side-effect profile for injectable GHK-Cu, so do not infer one from the topical study.

When to seek urgent care

  • Seek urgent care for a severe allergic reaction. Signs include trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, hives and dizziness.

[1] [2]

4. How it has been studied

The study was randomized and controlled. It took place after carbon dioxide laser resurfacing around the mouth, and 13 people completed the two topical-regimen groups.

That design makes the setting specific. Participants used whole regimens rather than a single isolated ingredient, and the follow-up reached 12 weeks.

[1]

5. What the evidence actually shows

At 12 weeks, satisfaction favored the GHK-Cu regimen. Objective evaluations did not find a significant between-group difference in redness resolution, wrinkles or overall skin quality.

So the study supports a satisfaction signal in this setting, not a proven objective advantage. It also does not establish benefit for routine cosmetic use outside post-procedure care or for injectable GHK-Cu.

[1]

6. Who should be cautious

People recovering from a procedure are the group the study directly covers. Ask the treating dermatologist before introducing products during post-procedure care.

Anyone with persistent irritation should raise it with a clinician. The supplied evidence does not identify other specific groups who should avoid GHK-Cu.

[1] [2]

7. Monitoring

The study measured satisfaction and objective skin outcomes such as redness resolution, wrinkles and overall skin quality at 12 weeks. Those are reasonable things to watch in a similar setting.

The supplied evidence does not set out a monitoring routine for injectable GHK-Cu, so treat any such routine as outside what these studies show.

[1]

8. Food and muscle

The supplied evidence does not cover food interactions or muscle effects for GHK-Cu. The study looked at skin outcomes after a procedure, not diet or muscle measures.

This is a narrow gap rather than a finding of no effect. The topical study simply did not test those areas.

[1]

9. Timing and interruptions

The study ran to a 12-week assessment, but the supplied evidence gives no timing instructions and no missed-dose rules. Those belong to the exact product and its instructions.

For a prescription product, follow the instructions for that exact product and ask a pharmacist or prescriber about interruptions.

[1]

10. Common mistakes

A common mistake is treating the topical result as proof for injectable GHK-Cu. Another is assuming one formulation speaks for all topical products.

A third is reading the satisfaction result as an objective win. Objective evaluations did not find a significant between-group difference, so both parts of the result matter.

[1]

11. FAQ

Does the study prove GHK-Cu works?
It shows a satisfaction signal after a procedure. Objective evaluations did not find a significant between-group difference, so the proof is partial.
Can I apply the results to injectable GHK-Cu?
Not directly. The study used topical regimens, and the supplied evidence gives no injectable use or timing instructions.
Does it work for everyday cosmetic use?
The study took place after laser resurfacing around the mouth. Routine cosmetic use outside that setting was not tested.
Which product instructions should I follow?
The ingredient list and instructions for the exact topical product. For a prescription product, ask a pharmacist or prescriber.

12. Plain-English glossary

Tripeptide
A short chain of three amino acids.
Randomized controlled study
A study where people are assigned by chance to comparison groups.
Objective evaluation
A measured assessment rather than a personal satisfaction rating.
Topical regimen
A set of products applied to the skin, rather than a single ingredient.
